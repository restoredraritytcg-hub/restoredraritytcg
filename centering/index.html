How it works

Centering

The card is found, flattened and straightened (a tilted phone photo becomes a flat, square-on picture). Along each of the four borders, 11 points are measured: where the card's cut edge is (gold) and where the border's inner edge is (cyan). A straight line is fitted through each set; points that don't fit — art running into the border, glare, a nick — are left out (red). Left/right = left border ÷ (left + right), measured at the middle of the card; the same for top/bottom.

Tilt (cant)

If the printed frame is turned against the card's cut edges, the borders get wider at one end and narrower at the other. Centering IQ compares the angle of each inner line with its cut edge, averages the four, and shows how much each border changes end to end. Graders don't publish a tilt limit, but they judge the centering where it's worst — so a tilted card can read 55/45 at the top and 62/38 at the bottom. As a guide: under 0.15° is straight; 0.4°–0.8° is a slight tilt you can see side by side; over 1.5° is obvious.

Margin of error (±)

Comes from how well the points line up and the picture's resolution. A good phone photo or a 600 dpi scan is within about ±0.5; a small listing photo can be ±2 or more. When the margin straddles a grade line, the grade shows "on the line".

Grades

Limits from PSA's and CGC's published grading standards and Beckett's grading scale (front and back). It's the best grade the centering alone allows — corners, edges and surface decide the rest. For Beckett, this is the centering subgrade.

Toploaders and sleeves

Switch on "in a toploader or sleeve": the holder's outline is found first, then the card inside it. Glare streaks over a border are detected and those points left out — if a whole border is glared, tilt the card a little away from the light.

Privacy

Pictures never leave your device. A grade report sends only the grader, grades, measured centering, tilt and the picture type.
